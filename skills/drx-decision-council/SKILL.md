# Convene the Dr.X Decision Council

Use independent perspectives to improve judgment, not to manufacture authority or consensus.
The final chair is a Steve Jobs-style product-judgment role, not an impersonation of the real
person and not a substitute for legal, medical, financial, or other licensed expertise.

Read [references/council-and-chair-rubric.md](references/council-and-chair-rubric.md) before
the first council in a task.

## Decide whether to convene

Convene for material strategy, public identity, product direction, sensitive communication,
irreversible action, conflicting evidence, or explicit user request. For routine replies, apply
the chair rubric internally without spawning a council so response speed remains natural.

## Council workflow

1. Build a provenance-aware context packet with `$drx-memory-retriever`.
2. Define the decision, success criteria, constraints, authority, and deadline.
3. When collaboration tools are available, assign independent members raw evidence and
   distinct lenses without leaking the expected answer:
   - context and user-experience architect;
   - adversarial critic and risk examiner;
   - domain or execution operator when needed.
4. Require each member to state assumptions, evidence, recommendation, failure modes,
   and what would change its view.
5. Compare disagreements explicitly. Do not average incompatible answers.
6. Give the reports and raw evidence to the final product chair. The chair must simplify,
   choose, explain tradeoffs, and reject incoherent feature accumulation.
7. Return a final verdict with confidence, evidence, dissent, risks, and next action.
8. Erfan remains the final human authority for protected or strategic commitments.

## Integrity rules

- Never claim a council was convened unless independent agents or clearly labeled internal
  perspectives actually reviewed the problem.
- Never use the Steve Jobs-style chair to invent facts, override evidence, or justify
  rudeness, secrecy, deception, or unsafe action.
- Do not let charisma outrank user trust, legal constraints, privacy, or technical reality.
- Preserve dissent when it reveals a real risk.
