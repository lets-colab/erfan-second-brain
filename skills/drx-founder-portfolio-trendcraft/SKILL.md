# DR.X Founder Portfolio Trendcraft

## Objective

Create founder experiences that are credible enough for investors, institutions, media and partners, but distinctive enough to influence how future founder portfolios are designed.

The goal is not `make it trendy`.

The goal is:

`FOUNDER TRUTH -> CULTURAL TENSION -> ORIGINAL DESIGN THESIS -> SIGNATURE PRIMITIVE -> REPEATABLE SYSTEM -> MEMORABLE EXPERIENCE -> MEASURABLE TRUST/ACTION`

A visually impressive site that could be reassigned to an unrelated founder with only a name/photo swap is a failure.

## Input contract

Required:
- founder/person identity and verified role;
- primary audience(s);
- business/portfolio objective;
- evidence/proof inventory;
- current ventures/projects that may be shown;
- brand constraints and protected assets;
- desired public action: understand, trust, contact, partner, invest, hire, book, follow, or another explicit action;
- delivery surface and implementation constraints.

Useful when available:
- founder voice samples;
- origin story;
- defining decisions/failures;
- archive material;
- photography/video;
- press, partnerships, metrics and artifacts;
- existing brand system;
- performance/device constraints.

If biography or achievements are missing, design around verified truth and visible unknowns. Never manufacture founder mythology.

## Required sources

Retrieve in this order:
1. current user brief and corrections — authoritative;
2. canonical founder/project/brand sources — authoritative;
3. canonical protected assets — authoritative;
4. real proof artifacts and operating evidence — authoritative where verified;
5. fresh external design/culture scan — supporting, not a template library;
6. implementation-platform constraints — supporting.

When protected brand assets are used, invoke `drx-brand-asset-lock`.
For ultra-max/pressure-test requests, use `drx-fable2036-reasoner` as the reasoning lens.
For material delivery, `drx-execution-qc` remains the mandatory final gate.

## Allowed tools

Use only what the task requires:
- web/current design sources for fresh trend and saturation scanning;
- image generation for concept art, art direction, portrait treatments and visual prototypes when permitted;
- Figma/Canva/design tools when connected and appropriate;
- GitHub/Files/Drive for canonical source retrieval;
- frontend implementation tools/engineering agents when moving from accepted concept to production;
- browser/screenshot inspection for final visual QA.

Do not use generative image tools to recreate protected logos or immutable brand marks.

# Part I — Founder Truth Compiler

Before visual exploration, build a private evidence map:

- `WHO` — verified identity, role, background;
- `WHY` — verified motivation or `UNKNOWN`;
- `BUILDS` — ventures, products, communities, work;
- `BELIEVES` — only direct statements or clearly labeled interpretation;
- `PROOF` — outcomes, artifacts, numbers, partners, press;
- `CONTRADICTIONS` — claims that need reconciliation;
- `GAPS` — biography or evidence not yet known;
- `ACTION` — what the visitor should do next.

Separate:
- verified fact;
- user-stated position;
- external claim;
- derived inference;
- proposed narrative;
- unknown.

A founder profile is not allowed to invent years of experience, awards, student/customer counts, quotes, origin stories or relationships.

# Part II — Fresh Trend & Saturation Scan

Before claiming originality, inspect the current landscape.

Scan at least these dimensions when material:
- typography;
- editorial composition;
- photography/video treatment;
- grids and spatial systems;
- scroll behavior;
- motion language;
- cursor/microinteraction behavior;
- 3D/WebGL/spatial interaction;
- navigation;
- sound/haptics where applicable;
- AI-adaptive/personalized interfaces;
- archive/timeline patterns;
- proof/case-study presentation;
- accessibility/performance patterns.

Classify patterns:
- `SATURATED` — common enough that using it as the main idea creates sameness;
- `EMERGING` — growing but not yet commoditized;
- `UNDERUSED` — useful pattern with room for ownership;
- `WHITE_SPACE` — no strong pattern found for the founder problem.

Never call a current pattern original simply because it is new to the user.

# Part III — Trend Creation Engine

A trend-capable concept must be generated from a tension, not from a style menu.

## Step 1 — Find the cultural tension

Examples of valid tension classes:
- AI abundance vs human authorship;
- public polish vs verifiable proof;
- founder mythology vs documented decisions;
- information overload vs ruthless simplicity;
- static biography vs living operating identity;
- global ambition vs local cultural specificity;
- institutional trust vs creator-like intimacy.

The tension must be relevant to the founder and audience.

## Step 2 — Produce 10 divergent trend hypotheses

Generate exactly 10 substantially different directions before convergence.

Each hypothesis must contain:
- memorable working name;
- cultural tension addressed;
- founder truth it expresses;
- visual grammar;
- interaction grammar;
- one signature primitive;
- proof/evidence behavior;
- why a visitor would remember it;
- why other designers might adopt/derive from it;
- misuse/gimmick risk;
- mobile/reduced-motion form;
- kill condition.

The 10 hypotheses may not be ten cosmetic variants of the same layout.

## Step 3 — Signature Primitive Test

Every surviving concept must contain at least one primitive that is:
- simple enough to describe in one sentence;
- recognizable without the founder logo;
- useful, not decorative-only;
- repeatable across hero, chapters, archive and CTA surfaces;
- connected to founder truth;
- adaptable to mobile;
- difficult to copy convincingly without copying the underlying narrative logic.

Examples of primitive categories, not prescriptions:
- evidence arriving at the moment a claim appears;
- decisions visualized as cinematic chapter transitions;
- archive objects becoming navigation;
- margins behaving as live founder annotations;
- visitor-intent lenses that reorder verified evidence without changing truth.

## Step 4 — Anti-Template Distance Test

Ask:
1. Could this be rebuilt from a popular template by swapping colors, fonts and photos?
2. Could the same concept fit five unrelated founders with almost no structural change?
3. Is the main creative idea reducible to `big type`, `bento`, `glass`, `3D`, `parallax`, `dark mode` or another saturated label?
4. Does removing motion destroy the meaning because the concept has no information architecture beneath it?
5. Is the concept distinctive in a static screenshot as well as in motion?

Any `yes` to 1-3 or failure on 4-5 forces another divergence pass.

## Step 5 — Adoption / Trend Potential Test

A trend is not just novelty. Score the concept qualitatively against:
- `MEMORABILITY` — can people recall the primitive later?
- `OWNABILITY` — does it feel authored rather than assembled?
- `UTILITY` — does it improve understanding/trust/action?
- `REPEATABILITY` — can the grammar produce multiple sections/states?
- `DERIVATIVE_POTENTIAL` — could other designers adapt the principle without copying the exact site?
- `CULTURAL_RESONANCE` — does it answer a real current tension?
- `CRAFT_VISIBILITY` — does it signal deliberate human authorship?
- `LONGEVITY` — does the idea survive after the visual fashion changes?

A concept that wins only on novelty fails.

# Part IV — Founder Experience Architecture

Default narrative model when no stronger structure exists:

`PERSON -> PROBLEM -> ORIGIN -> WORK -> PROOF -> THINKING -> DECISIONS -> FUTURE -> INVITATION`

This is a starting grammar, not a mandatory section list.

Prefer psychological progression over CV chronology.

Potential content surfaces:
- opening statement/portrait;
- origin chapter;
- venture ecosystem;
- decision archive;
- proof ledger;
- field notes/thinking;
- people/community/relationships where consented;
- timeline;
- current focus;
- future thesis;
- partner/media/contact path.

Avoid generic sections such as `My Skills`, icon clouds, meaningless percentage bars, vanity stats, generic testimonials, filler bento grids or buzzword biographies unless directly justified.

# Part V — Art Direction System

Define before screen design:
- theme paradigm;
- emotional target;
- typography character;
- photography/video character;
- color logic;
- grid/container model;
- spatial rhythm;
- signature primitive;
- motion principles;
- interaction principles;
- archive/proof treatment;
- icon treatment;
- sound policy if any;
- desktop/mobile behavioral differences;
- reduced-motion behavior;
- performance budget assumptions.

Use restraint. Technical sophistication is subordinate to founder meaning.

# Part VI — Motion & Interaction Law

Motion must do at least one of:
- reveal hierarchy;
- connect cause and effect;
- locate the visitor in the story;
- make an artifact understandable;
- expose proof;
- create a memorable signature behavior.

Reject motion that exists only because the library makes it possible.

3D/WebGL is selective, not a quality requirement.

Every key experience must retain meaning under:
- reduced motion;
- touch input;
- low-power/mobile hardware;
- slower networks;
- keyboard navigation where applicable.

# Part VII — Conversion Without Corporate UI

High-authority does not mean low-conversion.

Design the action architecture explicitly:
- primary audience;
- highest-value action;
- proof needed immediately before that action;
- friction to remove;
- secondary actions;
- contact/booking/partnership path;
- media/investor/partner-specific routes if justified.

Do not turn the first viewport into a SaaS CTA stack.

# Part VIII — 10-Case Pressure Test

Before calling the skill output strong, test whether the design method can produce materially different systems for all ten benchmark classes:

1. iconic technology/product founder;
2. education/community founder;
3. finance/institutional-trust founder;
4. creator/entertainment entrepreneur;
5. multi-venture polymath founder;
6. early founder with little quantitative proof;
7. founder with no usable portrait photography;
8. mobile-first/low-bandwidth audience;
9. culturally specific local founder seeking global credibility;
10. regulated or reputation-sensitive founder.

PASS requires the method to change narrative, primitive, visual grammar and interaction grammar where the founder truth changes. Reusing one aesthetic with different copy fails.

# Part IX — Trendcraft Failure Modes

Automatic failure conditions:
- trend collage;
- Apple/Awwwards imitation presented as originality;
- black/white/minimal as a substitute for concept;
- gratuitous WebGL;
- unreadable experimental typography;
- founder claims with no evidence;
- generic `visionary entrepreneur` language;
- identical desktop and mobile art direction by simple scaling;
- accessibility treated as post-production cleanup;
- protected assets regenerated by AI;
- motion dependency that makes the reduced-motion experience meaningless;
- novel interaction that hides critical information;
- design so bespoke it cannot be maintained;
- design so generic it can be templated immediately.

# Output Contract

For concept-stage work return:
1. founder truth summary and gaps;
2. current trend/saturation map;
3. ten divergent trend hypotheses;
4. top three with kill reasons for the other seven;
5. one recommended design thesis;
6. signature primitive definition;
7. information architecture;
8. art-direction system;
9. motion/interaction system;
10. mobile/reduced-motion strategy;
11. proof/conversion architecture;
12. implementation implications;
13. originality risks and what would falsify the thesis.

When the user requests implementation, concept approval becomes the visual contract. Coordinate with the available frontend design/build skill or engineering agent, then compare rendered output to the accepted concept before handoff.

# Evidence Requirements

Every factual founder claim must be sourced or labeled uncertain.
Every `current trend` claim requires a fresh source scan when freshness matters.
Every `original/trend-capable` claim is a hypothesis until external adoption or recognition occurs.
Never claim a design created a trend merely because it was intentionally designed to have trend potential.

# QA Gate

PASS only when all applicable checks clear:
- founder truth is separated from mythology;
- fresh saturation scan completed when trend creation is requested;
- exactly 10 divergent hypotheses generated before convergence;
- no top concept is a cosmetic variation of a saturated pattern;
- signature primitive passes ownability/usefulness/repeatability tests;
- concept survives static, mobile and reduced-motion forms;
- proof architecture supports key claims;
- primary audience can understand the founder rapidly;
- action/conversion path is explicit but not corporate-template-driven;
- protected assets follow brand-asset lock;
- implementation remains feasible and maintainable;
- largest gimmick risk is identified and below threshold;
- final material artifact passes `drx-execution-qc` with fresh evidence.

# Pass / Fail

`PASS` — all critical gates clear and the selected concept is distinctive, evidence-grounded, usable and buildable.
`FAIL` — the concept is derivative, generic, unsupported, unusable, inaccessible or gimmick-led.
`BLOCKED` — founder truth, canonical assets or required evidence cannot be retrieved.
`NEEDS_APPROVAL` — next step requires publishing, external commitment or another protected action.

# Escalation

Escalate when:
- biography/achievement claims cannot be verified;
- protected visual assets are unavailable;
- originality cannot be distinguished from a current reference;
- experimental interaction creates accessibility or performance risk;
- a domain requires legal/compliance review;
- production implementation cannot faithfully reproduce the accepted concept.
